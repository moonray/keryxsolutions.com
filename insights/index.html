Direct answers on AI agent engineering.

Short answers to the questions that come up when AI agents do real work: detecting off-track runs, versioning agents and their harnesses, transactional writes, multi-agent reliability, guardrails, and operations that survive the people who built them. Written from building and operating these systems at Keryx Solutions.

How do you detect when an AI agent workflow goes off track?

Give every step an observable contract: the artifact it must produce, the budget it must stay within, and the assertions its result must pass before the run reports success. Read more…

How do you version and update AI agents without breaking workflows?

Keep agent definitions in version control as one reviewable unit, pin the model and harness, gate changes behind contract tests, and roll out in stages. Read more…

How can several database updates from an AI workflow succeed or fail together?

Plan the work first, then wrap the writes in a single transaction that commits everything or nothing, with idempotency keys so retries stay safe. Read more…

What setup patterns keep multi-agent research workflows from breaking midway?

One orchestrator owns the plan, every delegated step is bounded and verifiable, and progress persists so failed runs resume instead of restarting. Read more…

How do you evaluate Smithery for agent automation?

Judge any agent-tooling platform on the contract it exposes, the trust surface it adds, and what happens when a tool changes. Read more…

How do companies keep AI agents from taking the wrong action in treasury workflows?

Tiered autonomy: read-only by default, scoped credentials, API-enforced limits, and approval gates on anything that moves money. Read more…

What makes an AI platform an execution layer instead of another dashboard?

The test: does it perform the work across your existing tools, with encoded runbooks and verifiable actions, or just display them better? Read more…

How do you make campaign operations resilient to operator departures?

Move the sequencing logic, optimization patterns, and workflow decisions into the stack as encoded, runnable workflows instead of keeping them in whoever runs them. Read more…
