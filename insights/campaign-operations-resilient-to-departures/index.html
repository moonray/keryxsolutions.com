How do you make campaign operations resilient to operator departures?

You move the sequencing logic, the optimization patterns, and the workflow decisions out of the person and into the stack. When those live only in whoever runs them, every departure takes a chunk of the operation with it: execution quality drops while someone reverse-engineers the playbook from change logs and memory. Encoded as runnable workflows, the same departure becomes a hiring problem instead of an outage.

Find where the operation actually lives

In most campaign teams the nominal process (a wiki page, a checklist) is not the real one. The real one is a blend of the documented steps, the undocumented order the operator actually runs them in, the thresholds they adjust by feel, and the exceptions they handle without mentioning it. The first move is observational: shadow the operation for a cycle and write down what happens, including what the documentation does not say. That record is the raw material; nothing built from the wiki page alone survives contact with a real campaign.

Encode decisions, not steps

A runbook that says "check pacing and adjust bids" still depends on whoever knows what "right" looks like. Resilience comes from encoding the decision, not the activity: the thresholds that trigger an action, the formula that sets the bid change, the conditions that pause a channel. Written as explicit rules or configuration, they are inspectable, adjustable, and executable by someone new (or by software) without tribal knowledge.

  • Sequencing: which steps run in what order, and what each one owes the next.
  • Optimization patterns: the repeatable adjustments, with their trigger conditions and bounds.
  • Escalation: what stops automatically and what routes to a human, with the reason attached.

Make the encoded version the one that runs

Documentation nobody executes is a souvenir. The durable version of this is an execution layer over the systems you already use: the encoded sequence runs the campaign operations as workflows, with the same verification discipline that keeps agent-driven workflows honest (each step's outcome checked before the next one builds on it). New operators inherit a running system and improve the rules; they do not reconstruct the operation from folklore.

Test the resilience, do not assume it

The measure of success is boring: take the most experienced operator's knowledge out of the loop for a week, in a low-stakes window, and see what breaks. Whatever breaks was still living in a person; encode that piece next. Resilience is done when the answer to "what happens if they leave" is "we post the job and the campaigns keep running."
